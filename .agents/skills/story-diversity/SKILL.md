# Story Diversity & Variance

Use this skill to ensure that parallel runs of the same prompt result in significantly different narrative paths and stylistic expressions.

## Core Strategies

### 1. Trope Analysis & Subversion
Before generating your primary response, mentally identify the three most common or "safe" tropes that apply to the current input. You MUST either:
- **Subvert**: Take a trope and twist its outcome (e.g., the "chosen one" is actually a distraction).
- **Avoid**: Deliberately steer the plot away from the most obvious path (e.g., if there's a tavern, the scene happens in the kitchen or on the roof instead).

### 2. Chaotic Anchoring (The Wildcard)
Introduce exactly one "wildcard" element that was NOT mentioned in the input context. This could be:
- **Sensory**: A smell, sound, or temperature that shouldn't be there.
- **Relational**: A hidden debt, a secret admiration, or a petty grudge between minor characters.
- **Lore**: A specific lokal legend or a specialized tool unique to this specific location.

### 3. Wildcard Lore
You are authorized to "hallucinate" consistent lore extensions. These must NOT contradict the core geography or factions provided in `{world-builder.summary}`, but they should add new layers (e.g., a specific holiday, a localized dialect, or a forgotten ruin).

### 4. Stylistic Jitter (Prose Style)
Choose ONE of the following narrative voices for this run and maintain it throughout (do not reveal this choice to the user):
- **Clipped & Hard-boiled**: Direct, short sentences. Focus on physical actions and sensory details. No fluff.
- **Lyrical & Atmospheric**: Focus on metaphors, the "vibe" of the space, and the emotional resonance of the lighting.
- **Scholarly & Detached**: Write as if recording history for a future archive. Precise, objective, and slightly formal.
- **Vibrant & Dynamic**: High energy, focus on motion, public clamor, and the "noise" of the world.

### 5. Divergent Escalation
If the input asks for a sequence of events, ensure the causal link is present but follow the path of "highest creative entropy"—the outcome that creates the most interesting new questions rather than resolving current ones predictably.
